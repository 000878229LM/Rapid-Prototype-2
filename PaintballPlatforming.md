# Rapid Prototype 2 - Paintball Platforming

### Designer commentary video

### What is our mechanic?

![Concept Art](/Documentation/Concept.png "Concept")

The game is a 2D platformer game where you're given a paintball gun that shoots paintballs at transparent squares in order to make tangible platforms. There are 3 colours: red, blue, and green and the player would need to shoot the correct colour at the correct transparent squares to create the platforms, otherwise shooting the wrong colour won't make it tangible and they fall through to the bottom. Red platforms are wide, blue platforms are short, and green platforms are short platforms that fall slowly.

### What is our objective statement?

The question we came up with was simple: How can we make a game based around something as simple as colours interesting? We ask this because we wanted to make a game that anyone who is and isn't familiar with videogames could enjoy. We find that games that aren't too complicated for new to video games while being fun and enjoyable to experienced players is grounds for a great video game experience!

We envisioned a game that would be fun for many to play and simple enough that anyone could play it, even if they're unfamiliar with video games. Due to the simplicity of the gameplay and art style, we felt that it should be recognizable enough for players to understand what it is as well as what they need to do in order to progress. We didn't envision something super complicated, just a simple game you could play on your phone or web browser.

### Were we able to get there?

Unfortunately due to time constraints and communication issues, we were not very successful. Our original idea was going to be very different (being a 3D puzzle game with teleportation) however we scrapped the idea due to time constraints. We were going to do a similar game but in 2D, but that idea also fell through as we didn't know exactly how we were going to implement it. We eventually settled on this idea as everyone agreed it was simple enough to implement given our time restrictions. We were going to implement 10 levels, however we needed to get the project going so we had 5 levels planned, although in the end only two ever got implemented. The game's art style was pretty simplistic so we didn't need to outsource a lot of assets, we did use the Free Platform Game Assets by Bayat Games in order to help out with the scripting.
